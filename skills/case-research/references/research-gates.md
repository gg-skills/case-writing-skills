# Research readiness gates

Apply each gate only when it bears on the author's decision and the sources
available for that case. Record the decision, date or period, teaching tension,
and comparison set when known. A missing date does not by itself block a case
whose decision is bounded by an event or period. Do not invent any of these.

Keep the pack partial when evidence needed to understand or analyze the chosen
decision is missing. Name the open gate in `history[].next_action`, retain the
last successful `stage`, and set `next_skill: "case-research"`. Record why a
conditional gate does not apply. A confirmed decision with the applicable
gates satisfied can hand off to `case-business-case`.

## 1. Decision and contemporary context

Identify who decides, the available options, the stakes, and the time boundary.
For a historical decision, seek sources available at that time. Search press
near the decision when public reaction matters; a same-day or prior-fortnight
objection is useful when it exists, not a universal prerequisite. File an
outside objection under `the outside objection` only when a source actually
raises one. Otherwise record the sourced tension that can open the case.

## 2. Changes between sources and decision

When a material fact may have changed between a filing or interview and the
decision, search that gap and record both dates, the later sourced value, and
any unresolved uncertainty. Do not demand two observations for a stable fact
or a case without periodic filings.

## 3. Decision evidence and exhibits

The pack must let students compare the real options. For a quantitative
economic choice, prepare an exhibit with the inputs and arithmetic needed to
calculate the consequence of each option. A segment income statement alone
does not establish those consequences. For a qualitative choice, prepare the
relevant evidence and trade-offs without inventing numbers.

For an asset-light contract versus a capital-intensive alternative, record
financing, timing control, revenue shares, caps, and a worked calculation when
those terms are material and supported by sources.

## 4. Comparison set

When `contrast_set` is set, test each author-named item on the dimensions
that matter to the decision. Control, performance against cost, participation,
and downstream effects may be useful for a portfolio decision; do not require
irrelevant fields such as fame or downstream categories in another case.
Record missing decision-relevant comparisons as gaps.

## 5. Industry or operating context

Extract the market, rivals, regulation, or organizational context necessary
to understand the choice. Market size, firm share, named rivals, and an
upcoming adjacent product are required only when they materially affect this
decision and can be established from sources. Mark unavailable material facts
as gaps rather than filling them with guesses.

## 6. Decision-relevant voices

When a decision-relevant person has no primary words, mark the voice
`[MISSING]` and record it as a gap in `extraction-log.md`, with the kind
of source that could close it, such as an interview. Do not stop to ask:
the gap is presented to the author after the case, as
[`pipeline-flow.md`](pipeline-flow.md#gaps-in-the-evidence) says. Do not
write a sentence for that person. The gate is open only while that silence is
unmarked; an interview is not required to complete the pack. A person with
primary words needs no `[MISSING]` mark.

## 7. Asset structure and operating heads

When sources describe an asset as families, clusters, or a system, capture
that structure rather than reducing it to a headcount. When the decision spans
several businesses and a source names an operating head, include the person
and a sourced line on how that business makes money. Mark a missing role or
line `[MISSING]` and record it as a gap; do not invent it. These checks do not
apply when the decision does not use that structure or those businesses.

## Handoff to narrative writing

Before completing the pack, identify the sourced opening tension, the
arguments for the live options, and any decision-relevant gaps. When an
outside objection is the intended opening, record the sourced responses
available to answer it. Do not label the pack ready for a required opening
that lacks its supporting evidence. The narrative may choose another
evidence-backed opening when the objection or responses do not exist.
