# Hybrid form: decision, context, and sourced narrative

The case body uses a hybrid form that combines two registers. The point
is to keep the case readable as a teaching instrument while keeping
students engaged.

A missing voice is `[MISSING]` and a question to the author, not a
reconstructed quote from a published teaching case, a later interview, or
a character sketch.

## Opening and structure

Open with a sourced decision tension, develop the options and their context,
and end before the outcome. The A/B/C structure and proportions are optional
planning aids. The specific objection, five-beat business walk, and
calculable exhibit below apply when the case's decision and evidence support
them. Use narrative prose when scene details or direct quotations are
unavailable.

## Opening gate

When a contemporary outside objection and responses are documented, a useful
opening shows, in this order:

1. An outside objection, from a same-day or prior-fortnight source, that names the doubt.
2. At least two sourced replies.
3. Only then, the protagonist's questions.

If that pattern is chosen, do not put analytical questions before the
objection and responses. If the responses are absent, do not invent them:
use another sourced opening, or return to research if those responses are
necessary to the author's chosen design. In every form, establish who faces
the decision, its stakes, and the available options early.

A passing shape, with no quotation marks because the words must come from
the pack: on the decision morning a newspaper raises the doubt about the
lead product and the smaller products named for comparison. Two insiders
answer in words that paper, or another primary source, already printed.
Only after those answers does the narrator state the protagonist's
questions.

A different valid shape: an evidenced meeting or deadline establishes the
decision and stakes without a press objection.

## Five beats

For a decision that compares businesses, walk each relevant business in five
short beats where the evidence supports them:

1. What it sells.
2. Who buys.
3. How it reaches them.
4. How it has performed.
5. What the industry looks like.

Each factual beat is sourced. A margin table does not replace the walk. The
fifth beat uses an industry slice when it affects the decision. When the pack describes the asset as
families, clusters, or a system, the case uses that structure. A headcount
is not a strategy.

## Ending gate

Restate the live options and the strongest sourced argument for each. A
sourced conviction may remain when alternatives stay analyzable and the
outcome is not told. Do not narrate the historical outcome in a decision case.

## Calculable exhibit

For a quantitative economic choice, include an exhibit that lets students
compute the consequence of each option using numbers printed in the case.
If the required inputs are missing, return to research. For a qualitative
choice, present the evidence needed to compare options without invented
calculations. Filing tables may sit in an appendix; a segment income
statement alone does not establish the consequences of an economic choice.

When the choice is an asset-light contract versus a capital-intensive
alternative, the exhibit states who finances, who sets the date, what
share of the primary revenue and of the downstream revenue each party
keeps, and any cap, and it includes one worked example.

## Two clocks

When a material fact changed between the last source and the decision, the
case uses the later sourced fact and shows its date and source.

## Two registers

| Register | Use it for | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Academic context | Sectoral, regulatory, or market context where the decision needs it. | "Brazilian fintech regulation in 2019 was fragmented across the Central Bank and the CVM. New entrants faced..." |
| Sourced scene | The protagonist's situation or decision moment when scene details are evidenced. | "Alice reviewed the spreadsheet before the documented board meeting." |

The scene example is a register sample, not a license to invent a scene.

## Optional Part A — Context

- Tone: third-person, descriptive, neutral.
- Dialogue is optional. The reader sees a sourced translation in the case language; the verbatim original may follow as `*Original:*`.
- Establishes: sector, regulation, market structure, key actors, including the protagonist where useful.
- Can precede or follow the opening exchange according to the author's brief and the evidence.

## Optional Part B — Dilemma + Data

- Tone: third-person narrative with sourced dialogue when available.
- Walks each relevant business in the five beats when the decision compares businesses.
- Each exhibit (table, figure) answers a question the protagonist is asking at that moment.
- Exhibits are referenced inline as `![Exhibit N — description (source)](assets/exhibit-N.png)` once assets are extracted; until then, reference them as `see Exhibit N below`. Write the label in the case language from [`taxonomy.md`](taxonomy.md): in Portuguese, `![Anexo N — descrição (fonte)](assets/exhibit-N.png)`, "ver Anexo N", and an "Anexos" section. The file name stays in English.
- Includes a calculable exhibit for a quantitative choice. Filing tables may sit in an appendix.

## Optional Part C — Decision Moment

- Tone: third-person narrative, scene-driven.
- Brings students to the choice they must analyze.
- Uses sourced narrative or dialogue as the evidence permits.
- Restates the live options, with sourced arguments for each.
- A sourced conviction may remain when alternatives stay open and the outcome is not told.

## What the form is NOT

- **Not a literature review.** Theory, frameworks, and prior literature live in the teaching note.
- **Not a case study of success/failure.** The case must show the decision. The outcome is not told.
- **Not a transcript of interviews.** Direct quotes anchor voices; they are not the spine.
- **Not a press release.** The protagonist has a real dilemma with no clean answer.
- **Not a filing summary.** A segment income statement does not replace the decision analysis or a calculable exhibit when the choice is quantitative.

## Example of a register (context) — fictional

> Brazilian retail credit grew 18% per year between 2018 and 2023, outpacing GDP growth by a factor of four. The expansion was uneven: large incumbents captured 70% of new originations, while a long tail of regional players competed for thin margins and questionable borrowers. Acme, founded in 2011 in the interior of Minas Gerais, was one of the regional players. Its founder, a former bank-branch manager, had spent the previous decade building a network of small-city agents who could underwrite loans that the incumbents' algorithms rejected.

This sample shows sector context. A finished opening must also establish the
protagonist's decision, stakes, and live options from evidence.

## Example of a register (scene) — fictional

> By March 2023, the spread between Acme's funding cost and its average yield had compressed from 14 percentage points to 9. Mariana (CFO), whose interview states the compression, brought the founder two sourced views of the premium book: the book as it stood, and the book without its worst 10% of borrowers.

This is a scene only because the pack already holds the place, the people,
and the figures. The exhibit that follows shows those two views. Do not
add a gesture, a room, or a line the pack does not contain.

## Tension management

- **Open on sourced tension.** Use an outside objection and responses when documented and useful; otherwise choose another evidenced opening.
- **Do not tell the outcome.** A sourced conviction may remain when the alternatives stay analyzable.
- **Use sourced disagreement as tension.** When two sources disagree, the disagreement is the case.

## Quote anchoring

Every direct quotation traces to a verbatim quote in
`research/quotes-by-theme.md` or in the manual brief. The reader meets
that sentence in the language of the case. The highlighted quotation is
the translation. When the source uses another language, the verbatim
sentence may follow on the next line:

```markdown
> "Ninguém garante o resultado do lançamento, e uma continuação precisa superar o original."

*Original:* "No one can guarantee the launch, and a sequel has to outperform the original."
```

Omit `*Original:*` when the source is already in the case language, or
when the author asks to leave the source sentence off the page. A short
quotation inside a paragraph uses the same order: the words in quotation
marks are the case language, and `*Original:*` may follow in that
paragraph. Do not lead with the foreign-language sentence and then gloss
it. The translation adds no fact the source sentence does not state.
Follow [`reader-formats.md`](reader-formats.md).

If a source documents a position without direct speech, paraphrase it as
attributed indirect speech without quotation marks, and only when the
source itself states the position. Do not transplant an interview quote
into an invented meeting or invent gestures and locations.

A decision-relevant voice with no primary words is `[MISSING]`. Do not
draft the sentence. People who never speak about the decision stay in the
pack and out of the narrative.
