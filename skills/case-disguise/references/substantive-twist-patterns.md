# Substantive twist patterns

When the disguise goes beyond surface rotation — when the author wants
to lead the discussion to a different angle — apply one of the
substantive twist patterns. Each pattern has a trigger, a typical
shift, and a quality gate.

## Geography shift

- **Trigger**: author wants to make the case relevant to a different locale.
- **Typical shift**: Brazil → Mexico, US → UK, urban → rural.
- **What changes**: company name, regulator, currency, market context, locale-specific names.
- **What must stay**: the decision type, the protagonist archetype, the teaching objective.
- **Quality gate**: a reader familiar with the new locale would find no anachronism or factual error.

## Date shift

- **Trigger**: the case is dated (regulatory regime, market structure, technology) and the author wants to bring it forward or push it back.
- **Typical shift**: 2019 → 2024 (technology and regulation); 2024 → 2019 (legacy systems and pre-AI workflows).
- **What changes**: dates, regulatory references, market data, technological references.
- **What must stay**: the decision type, the protagonist's role and motivation, the teaching objective.
- **Quality gate**: a reader familiar with the period would find no anachronism (e.g., a 2019 case should not reference ChatGPT; a 2024 case should not ignore AI).

## Gender shift

- **Trigger**: the author wants to surface gender-related dynamics in the discussion, or to vary the demographic mix.
- **Typical shift**: protagonist gender, pronouns, names, family references (if any).
- **What changes**: character pronouns and names; descriptions that were gender-coded; potentially the antagonist's gender if it matters to the discussion.
- **What must stay**: the decision type, the protagonist's role, the teaching objective, all evidence.
- **Quality gate**: the case does not introduce gender stereotypes; the shift is consistent with the evidence.

## Combined shifts

- **Trigger**: the author wants to vary across multiple axes at once.
- **Typical shift**: geography + date (e.g., Brazilian fintech in 2019 → Mexican fintech in 2024).
- **What changes**: everything that any single shift changes.
- **What must stay**: the teaching objective, the decision type, the character archetypes.
- **Quality gate**: the variant teaches the same lesson under the new context.

## Anti-patterns

- **Twist the teaching objective**: defeats the purpose; the case becomes a new case, not a disguise.
- **Twist the decision type**: same risk.
- **Twist with no locale or period research**: the variant will have factual errors that the audience will catch.
- **Twist without re-checking exhibits**: any exhibit that depends on the twisted dimension must be recomputed or replaced.

## Worked example

**Original case**: Brazilian fintech in 2019, female CFO protagonist, decision about entering Argentina.

**Twist**: Mexican fintech in 2024, female CFO protagonist, decision about entering Brazil.

**What changes**: company name, regulator (CNMV → CVM), currency (ARS → BRL), market context (Argentine macro 2019 → Brazilian macro 2024), country references, exhibit 3 financial table.

**What stays**: female CFO archetype, decision type (geographic expansion), teaching objective (weighing regulatory risk vs market size).

**Audit trail row**: `Part A, page 2 — Brazilian fintech regulation 2019 → Mexican fintech regulation 2024 — locale shift`.
